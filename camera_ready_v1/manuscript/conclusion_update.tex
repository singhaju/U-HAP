Our newly-added update-latency comparison against OPA shows that
U-HAP's compilation-driven design pays off twice: on the steady-state
request path \emph{and} on the policy-change path. At 2000 rules,
U-HAP completes the full edit-to-first-decision cycle in 223\,ms
versus OPA's 611\,ms (2.73$\times$ end-to-end, 19.5$\times$ on the
engine-only post-parse path), with a compiled-state footprint of
2.7\,MiB. Combined with the per-request results of
Sections~\ref{sec:exp1}--\ref{sec:exp4}, this evidence supports the
claim that compile-time hash consing and indexed artifacts are the
right architectural choice for Kubernetes admission-time
authorization at production scale.
